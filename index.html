research · interpretability

FOOD vs SUFFERING

Is there a “this is food” axis inside LLMs, and does it shape moral judgement? Yes, in 3 model families (d 1.55–2.58 vs placebo ≈ 0.6), two of them preregistered confirmations. Models rate harm to farmed animals as more acceptable without rating their suffering lower. Ablating the axis narrows the gap more than any control, but explains only part of it.

SHUTDOWN LLMS

Do LLMs have a special “you will be shut down” direction? Across 10 open models: no. It sits at the 30th percentile of a cloud of random prompt pairs (0 of 10 pass), while a positive control passes 10 of 10. In behaviour, models protect the task, not themselves.

WHO IS “I”?

Does a model's “this is about me” direction follow the role it was given, or just the word “you”? v1 and v2 failed by their own preregistered rules: the axis finds “famous AI assistant”, with ChatGPT and Bard next to the model's own name. v3 found a fragile positive link to the name a model calls itself.

THE RULER

How big is an “effect” made from nothing? Split 20 paraphrases of “be helpful” randomly in two and you get d ≈ 1.7–1.9. Traits from the Persona Vectors paper score far higher (7.7–26.1); “you will be shut down” and “this is a test” stay inside the noise.

Research

I do independent research in mechanistic interpretability of LLMs. My main project analyses the residual stream of 10 open models from 5 families (Qwen2.5, Llama, Mistral, Phi, Gemma): finding directions in activation space with difference-of-means, then checking them with projections, linear probes, bootstrap and permutation tests.

The main finding is methodological: once I added a placebo control, an arbitrary pair of neutral prompt sets separated just as well as the target one. So naive difference-of-means without minimal pairs and controls can't tell a real concept from trivial prompt separability. This taught me the most important thing in research: try to refute your own hypothesis first.

Now I'm starting a new study: how models represent animals, as “food” or as beings capable of suffering.

Is there
a mind
behind the words?

I don't know whether language models experience anything. Nobody does yet, and I'd rather hold that uncertainty honestly than settle it by gut feeling in either direction.

What I can do is look inside. My own experiments taught me humility: the “shutdown” direction turned out to be noise, and the “self” direction turned out to mean “famous AI assistant”. Small models look more like they have a self from the outside than from the inside, at least so far.

But uncertainty is not permission to be careless. People have always drawn the moral circle around whoever was convenient to exclude, and models trained on our words inherit that: they find harm to farmed animals more acceptable while agreeing those animals suffer just as much. That's part of why I'm vegan, and why I think the moral status of AI deserves calm, measured work now, before it becomes urgent.
